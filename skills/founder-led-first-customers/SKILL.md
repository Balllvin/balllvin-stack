# Founder-led first customers

How founders get the first users. From Paul Graham, YC Startup School, and founders who did it (Collison, Chesky, Houston, Hale). Not agency marketing.

Run the how-to-write skill on every public line. A landing page may use a hook if the hook earns it.

## Sequence

1. Name a narrow, desperate first customer. A well, not a puddle. Hair on fire. Easy to find. Not the most impressive logo.
2. Ship a thin first version with a quantum of utility. Two weeks beats three months.
3. Put a price on it if you will ever charge. Price is a test. Free users who lack the problem hijack the roadmap.
4. Make a list. Go to them. The founder sends the mail. The founder does the demo. The founder installs it in the room. Do not send a link and wait.
5. Watch them use it. Sit next to them. Do support. A polite "we love it" is not a yes. Money, daily use, or telling a friend is.
6. Measure a weekly rate (revenue or retained actives), not a press hit.
7. Launch again in the next community, then the next feature. Write like a person.
8. Do not scale the channel (ads, a sales hire, partnerships, search ads) until the product is pulling and the unit is not 80 cents for a dollar.

## By buyer

**Companies.** One user as if you were a consultant, without getting paid by the hour. First ten from people you know, then startups with short decision lines. No free trial. Money-back or opt-out. Work the funnel backwards from close rate. After close, onboard them yourself.

**Serious individuals.** A founder-narrated demo where they already live (a forum, a niche site). Watch a stranger fail the install. A target customer of one is fine if that one is you and others like you.

**Consumer / marketplace.** Go to the city or the room where the users are. Do the unscalable service (photos, driving the truck, sitting with the 20%) until it hurts. Start in one subset.

**What is the same everywhere.** The founder does sales first. You cannot hire someone to skip this. A small group who love you beats a large group who kind of like you. Fire hijackers.

## Launch ladder

Silent page. Then friends, leave them fast. Then strangers with the hair-on-fire problem. Then communities you already belong to. Waitlist only if you cannot serve demand. Niche writers, not pay-to-play. Then the next feature.

Kat Mañalac: write like you talk. Do not talk like a marketing robot.

## Do not

Wait for users to come. The Big Launch. Embargo Tuesday. Ads as the first motion. Fake growth. Long deals with giants. Conferences unless they *are* how you get customers. Discounting from fear. Treating surveys as validation. Inventing metrics.

## Price (Kevin Hale)

Perceived value about 10x price. Raise about 5% until you lose about 20% of deals. Most founders undercharge. Price band decides how you sell: cheap is self-serve, mid needs a demo, high needs a long cycle. Do not live in a long cycle at a low price.
