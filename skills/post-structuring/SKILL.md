# How to structure posts

Topic pick is the post-topic-picking skill. Wording is the how-to-write skill. This skill is the order you cut the material.

Do these in order.

1. Structure the material into logical chunks. One object per chunk. Product is one comment. Market size is another. Multiple is another. Stance is another. Do not pile a fat parenthesis of extra stats onto the product sentence.
2. Condense each chunk to the core data that matters. Keep the mechanism and the number that carries it. Drop the rest of the research draft.
3. Simplify. Same meaning, fewer characters.
4. Only normal parentheses ( ). Never square brackets.

A chunk can be one comment or one block in a thread. A stranger should get that chunk without the others.

Social grammar can be loose. A missed full stop is fine. A comma where a period could go is fine.

## Worked example

The author already had a one-line stance on a company. Then they condensed the research into three comments.

Comment 1, product: what the company actually makes, in one sentence, plus one location fact, plus one recent operating number that carries the mechanism.

Comment 2, market and multiple: the end-market size and growth, then the valuation facts that survived. No product tour in this comment.

Comment 3, stance: they are long, and it may or may not work. No more data.

What a research draft often has, and what should survive:

- A product tour (three product names) becomes one sentence about the actual job the product does.
- A plant or office list becomes one compressed location fact.
- End-market stats leave the product comment and become their own comment. One object per chunk.
- Square brackets become ( ).
- Extra trailing multiples, cash figures, old-year sales, and peer names get cut. What stays: the forward multiple, one trailing sales multiple, a cash-flow multiple, and leverage if it matters.
- "Hard to know share" stays honest, not dressed up.
- The last comment is stance. No more data.
