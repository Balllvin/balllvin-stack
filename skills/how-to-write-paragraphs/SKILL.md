# How to write paragraphs

Sentence wording is the how-to-write skill (unslop). This skill is the piece: whether the paragraph and the essay move. Run it after unslop on anything longer than about four sentences.

Cohesion is sentence-to-sentence glue. Coherence is one picture. A paragraph can pick up the last word of every sentence and still walk off the topic. LLM drafts fail that way: locally fluent, globally stacked.

## Paragraph

- One idea, namable in five words. Two labels means split.
- Topic near the start. Middle develops it. End on a consequence the next paragraph can pick up. Do not recap sentence 1.
- Old then new in every sentence after the first. Open with something already in play. Put the new bit at the end. That ending becomes the next opening.
- Keep the same entity in topic position. Do not change the protagonist every sentence.
- Repeat the key words. Do not elegant-vary them. "This" needs a noun.
- A transition only when the logic actually turned. Cut thus / therefore / however / moreover / furthermore / in addition used as fake glue.
- Develop: point, evidence, why it matters. Do not stack three items or "Another… Also… Finally…"
- Vary sentence shape because the thought changed, not to sound punchy. Isolated one-liners that never connect are as bad as a run of identical sentences.
- One-sentence paragraphs only for a real turn.

## Essay / longform

- Each paragraph is a step, not a restart. The last sentence of N must make N+1 possible.
- Signpost by carrying a word, not by "This essay will," "First… Second…," "Furthermore," "In conclusion."
- Close by consequence or synthesis, not a recap. Never open a close with "in conclusion / in summary / in closing."
- Keep the main people and objects alive. Do not introduce a new cast every section.
- A section must change what the reader knows. Same claim in a new hat is not a section.
- Put actions in verbs and characters in subjects. Unpack noun stacks and leftover -ing ornaments after unslop.
- Do not open every piece with "X is a complex issue."

## Gotcha cap

Antithesis works once. A paragraph of "it's not X, it's Y" is the same move chewing itself.

- At most one gotcha, twist, or it's-X-not-Y pair per paragraph.
- At most one per section of about 400 words.
- After the contrast, the next sentence develops the positive claim. It does not flip again.
- Prefer saying what the thing is.
- Adjacent however / but / rather / instead / not…but is too dense. Keep one.

## Four-step edit (after unslop)

1. Unity. Five-word label per paragraph. Two labels: split. A sentence that does not serve the label: cut or move.
2. Given-new. If a sentence starts new and ends old, invert it. Keep the given stub short.
3. Handoff. Read only the last sentence of N and the first of N+1. If N+1 restarts the topic, rewrite N's close.
4. Density. Cut stacked contrasts, punch-line orphans, three-item stacks, scaffolding adverbs, recap closings, entities that appear once and vanish.

Stop when each paragraph has one job, one contrast at most, and a last sentence the next paragraph needs.
