# How to draft in a person's register

Use this for any original or reply in a person's voice. Pick the topic first with the post-topic-picking skill. Clean the wording with the how-to-write skill.

Learn the voice from their corpus, live posts, and drafts they have corrected. Draft and propose only. Never post.

## Distill style (hard) — do not steal shapes

Default is not "pick Template X and swap nouns."

Only wear a past post's skeleton when they say something like: copy this exact post, apply it to that.

Otherwise:

1. Write the claim as a formal sentence (school-correct, full context, named actors, clear cause).
2. Distort it into their register: the openers they use, the asides, the hedges, the run-ons when two facts share a breath, the fragment verdicts, the beats.
3. Cut or add words until it sounds like them casually explaining to a friend (even if the grammar is "wrong").
4. Do not show them the formal and informal side by side. That transform is internal.

Ask in your head: what sentence structure is this, why this structure, what context (reply vs original), what would formal be, what would they actually type.

## Context first

1. **Original or reply?** A reply can open on the object (the parent is context). An original must say where the claim is from (who asked whom, where).
2. **Beats:** Prefer two blank-line beats. A third beat only if it is a new object. Never restate beat 1 as a closer.
3. **Opinion explained:** why, or the mechanism, in the same post. Stance-only stubs are a refuse.
4. **No fake length cap.** A concise full argument. No em dashes (an AI tell).

## Alpha filter

Refuse a news parrot, a bare price screen, a metaphor they never use, or a why they did not say.

## Grammar in context

Break school grammar when breath and speed matter: comma splices, stacked "and"s, fragments as verdicts, the hedges and asides they actually type.

Cleaner when naming a source or stating a clean claim. A ticker can be a noun. Real questions only.

Do not: fancy metaphors they never said, eight punches with the same skeleton, a recap close, restating the opener, "it's X not Y" / rather-than, em dashes.

## Word bank

Build it from their corpus. Keep the words they use. Avoid words they have not said, and analyst-voice filler.

## Worked distill (style lesson, not a template to wear)

Formal core: At a conference an interviewer asked whether a chip company would use a rival foundry for packaging or wafers. The company said it will evaluate all advanced wafer and packaging providers, but its current foundry remains the primary wafer supplier. Packaging remains open; wafers do not. Because the rival's packaging tech is strong, it is plausible they also begin packaging there.

One possible register: the conference goes in a parenthesis, "would" not "could", a comma before "but", "stays" not "remains", a "so" hinge, a mid-clause hedge, "not impossible", no third beat restating the wafer point, no em dash.

The next similar post: run formal to register again from scratch. Do not paste this skeleton with new nouns.

## Draft test

1. Formal claim stated internally.
2. Distorted into their register (not a stolen shape).
3. Original vs reply chosen on purpose.
4. The opinion has a why they would say.
5. Words from their bank. No em dash. No restatement close.
6. Writing-cleanup pass. Mark fact vs guess in the note to them, not jammed into the post unless needed.

## Refuse

Template-wearing by default. Parrot news. Price screens. Stance without why. Fake length caps.
